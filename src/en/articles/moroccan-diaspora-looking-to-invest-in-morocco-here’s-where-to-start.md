For Moroccans living overseas, investing back home is no longer confined to property or traditional family businesses. Increasingly, entrepreneurs, professionals and executives are looking at Morocco as a place to launch companies, expand existing ventures or bring expertise and capital acquired abroad into new sectors.

Several mechanisms are available to support that ambition. Some have been designed specifically for Moroccans residing abroad, while others form part of Morocco’s broader drive to increase private investment.

One of the most prominent is **MDM Invest**. Revised in 2024, the scheme supports Moroccans abroad seeking to launch or expand a business in Morocco. Eligible projects can receive a **non-repayable contribution of up to 10% of total project costs, capped at MAD 5 million**. The minimum equity contribution has also been lowered to 20%, while the scope of eligible activities has widened to include sectors such as energy, transport, logistics, green industries and new technologies.

Financing is another part of the equation. **MDM Tamwil** was designed to help bridge that gap through a co-financing model involving the banking sector. According to Morocco’s Treasury and External Finance Directorate, the state-funded portion is provided at an **interest rate of 2.5%, excluding tax**. In practical terms, the scheme aims to make it easier for diaspora-led businesses to move from a viable project to a financed one.

But dedicated diaspora programmes are only part of the picture.

Moroccans living abroad may also qualify for incentives available under Morocco’s **Investment Charter**. Eligibility depends on the project itself: its size, the jobs it creates, its sector, location and, in some cases, its strategic importance.

This distinction matters. An investor based abroad should not necessarily begin by asking, “What support is available to the diaspora?” A more useful question may be: **“What support is available for my project?”**

Location also plays a role. Morocco’s **Regional Investment Centres (CRIs)** act as key entry points for investors, providing guidance on procedures, permits and available incentives. The opportunities and support available to an industrial project in Tangier may differ from those relevant to a technology company in Casablanca or a tourism venture in the Oriental region.

Investors transferring capital from abroad also need to consider foreign-exchange rules from the outset. When investments are financed in foreign currency in accordance with Moroccan regulations, Moroccans residing abroad can benefit from the convertibility regime applicable to foreign investment. Under the relevant conditions, this notably allows investment income and proceeds from a future sale or liquidation to be transferred abroad.

Keeping a clear record of how funds enter Morocco is therefore more than an administrative detail. Investors should check the applicable requirements with the **Foreign Exchange Office (Office des Changes)**, their bank and the relevant investment authorities before committing capital.

Morocco has no shortage of investment schemes. The real challenge for project holders abroad is knowing which ones apply to them and how to access them.

For the Moroccan diaspora, the starting point is therefore relatively straightforward: **define the project, understand the available incentives, identify the right regional and financial partners, and structure the investment before taking the first formal steps.**

Because what the diaspora can bring to Morocco extends beyond capital. It also includes expertise, international networks, technology, market access and experience accumulated across economies around the world.

*Programmes, amounts and eligibility requirements may change. Investors should verify the applicable terms with the relevant authorities before making an investment decision.*
